Incidentally we have never convinced ourselves that this is true


Consider any say real or complex vector space $V$ and any vector $\mathbf{v}\in V$. Then

$$
-1\cdot \mathbf{v} =-\mathbf{v}
$$

Where $-\mathbf{v}$ in the additive inverse of $\mathbf{v}$.

The axioms state that $1\cdot \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}$ but not the above

Try to prove this from the axioms of a vector space.
You may use the fact that $0\cdot \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}$ and that $0=1+ (-1)$
