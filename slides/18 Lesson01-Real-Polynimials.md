title: The Vector Space of Real Polynomials of maximum degree n

In this example we will not go through all the axioms in details but leave it as an exercise

Let the **set** $\mathbb{P}^n$ be all polynomials of max degree $n$ with **real** coefficients for some positive integer $n$.

That is   
$$
\mathbb{P}_n= \{ a_0x^0+a_1x^1+a_2x^2+...+a_nx^n|a_i\in \mathbb{R},x\in \mathbb{R} \}
$$
This **set** can be **turned into** a **real** vector space over the field $\mathbb{R}$ by exactly the same 
operations as the last example, namely **addition** and **scalar multiplication** of functions. Only now the scalars are real numbers and the functions are polynomials of degree at most $n$.

The only thing we need to check is that the result of 
these operations is still a polynomial of degree at most $n$. 

The sum of two polynomials of degree at most $n$ is again a polynomial of degree at most $n$, and multiplying a polynomial of degree at most $n$ by a real number does not change its degree.
