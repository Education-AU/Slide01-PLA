In the rest of the course we will be focusing on the **real** vector space $(\mathbb{R}^n,+,\cdot)$.

<br>
<br>
But occasionally come back to other vector spaces not to lose sight of the fact that $(\mathbb{R}^n,+,\cdot)$ is by no
means unique.
<br>
<br>

Notice the notation: $(\mathbb{R}^n,+,\cdot)$.
<br>
<br>

This is just to emphasize that a vector space is not just a set, but a set equipped with operations.
<br>
<br>

Also, we will not carry around the explicit $\oplus $ and $\odot $ operations anymore unless it is important to emphasize
these explicitly.
