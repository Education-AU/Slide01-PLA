If a basis exists for say $\mathbb{R}^2$ then our vector space calculations
can be performed on the coordinates of this basis.

Let a particular basis be denoted $\mathbf{b}_1$, $\mathbf{b}_2$

That is any  $\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in \mathbb{R}^2$ can be represented as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v} &= v_1\mathbf{b}_1+v_2\mathbf{b}_2\\
\mathbf{w} &= w_1\mathbf{b}_1+w_2\mathbf{b}_2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now consider the sum $\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}$ of $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w}&=v_1\mathbf{b}_1+v_2\mathbf{b}_2+w_1\mathbf{b}_1+w_2\mathbf{b}_2\\
&= (v_1+w_1)\mathbf{b}_1+ (v_2+w_2)\mathbf{b}_2
\end{aligned}
$$

From which we deduce that the sum of vectors can be calculated from the coordinates. Similarly for scalar multiplication

$$
\begin{aligned}
s\mathbf{v}&=s (v_1\mathbf{b}_1+v_2\mathbf{b}_2)\\
&=sv_1\mathbf{b}_1+sv_2\mathbf{b}_2
\end{aligned}
$$
