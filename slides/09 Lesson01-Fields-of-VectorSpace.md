In full generality, the definition of a vector space depends on 
the concept of a **field**. 

We will not study fields in detail

Informally, a field is a number system with two operations, addition($+$) 
and multiplication($\cdot$), satisfying certain rules and in which every 
nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse.

Well-known examples of fields are the 
1. The rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$,
2. The real numbers $\mathbb{R}$, and 
3. The complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$.
4. Finite prime fields $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Number systems that are **not** fields include 
1. The integers $\mathbb{Z}$
2. The natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$.
3. Finite non-prime fields $\mathbb{F}_{p^n}$ for $n>1$.

which are **rings** but not fields, since they do not have multiplicative inverses for all nonzero elements.
